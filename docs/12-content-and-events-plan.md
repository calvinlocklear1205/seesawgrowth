# Content, Events and Programmes

*How to put out content that books meetings. Research pulled 2026-09-08 with Firecrawl,
Exa and Perplexity.*

> **Dependency, flagged once:** Decision #1 (positioning) is still open, and content
> amplifies positioning — publishing at volume before it lands means either redoing the work
> or cementing the wrong message. **But the highest-value items here have long lead times**
> (webinar slots, 2027 judge applications, conference calls for speakers). Book and apply
> now; publish after. That split is the whole scheduling recommendation.

## 1. The filter: what "moves the dial" has to mean here

$1.5M ≈ 3 pods ≈ **4–6 genuinely qualified opportunities per quarter.** That number
changes what counts as good content, and it inverts most content-marketing advice.

At this volume, **reach is close to irrelevant and distribution precision is everything.**
A room of 12 hospice COOs beats 10,000 impressions, and a webinar registrant list of 125
named post-acute operators is worth more than a year of SEO traffic. So the ranking
question for any format is not "how many people see it" but:

1. **Does it borrow an audience that is already the ICP, or does it require building
   one?** Borrowing wins almost every time. SeeSaw does not have a distribution problem it
   can solve by publishing more; it has an access problem it can solve by renting someone
   else's list.
2. **Does it produce named people with titles**, or just a number in an analytics
   dashboard?
3. **Does it demonstrate judgement**, which is what a design-led studio actually sells,
   rather than describe capability?

Anything that optimises impressions, followers or raw lead count over
qualified-opportunity count is off-strategy. Several obvious formats fail this test and
are listed in §6 rather than quietly omitted.

## 2. Tier 1 — borrowed distribution. Start here.

### 2.1 Sponsored webinar with the trade press. The single highest-yield item.

**Hospice News and Home Health Care News sell sponsored webinars,** and their subscriber
base is precisely the Tier A/B surface in `03` and `11`. Both are part of the Aging Media
Network, which as of 2026-09-08 appears to be operating its events under the name
**Arrowfly** — worth confirming who to contact.

Published mechanics ([Hospice News](https://hospicenews.com/advertising/webinars/), read
2026-09-08):

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | 45-minute presentation, up to 3 panelists plus an HSPN moderator |
| Who does what | Client supplies the deck and speakers; HSPN manages production and promotes through its channels |
| Registrants | **~125 for a product/service-focused webinar; 250+ for trends or top-of-funnel topics** |
| Live attendance | **30–40% of registrants** |
| Afterwards | Recording sent to all registrants, including no-shows |
| Price | Not published — ask |

**Why this is the best item on the list.** Run the arithmetic against the target. A
product-focused webinar puts roughly 125 named hospice people with job titles on a list,
of whom 40–50 attend live. If even 4% are worth a real conversation, that is 5
conversations — **the entire quarterly target from one event.** And the registrant list is
the actual deliverable, not the attendance: it feeds directly into the LinkedIn motion in
`11` §4, and it is a list SeeSaw could not assemble any other way.

**Three things that decide whether it works:**

- **Co-present with a customer, not alone.** The strongest possible version has HPS on the
  panel saying "5x faster medication approvals" in their own voice. A vendor claiming a
  number is marketing; an operator claiming it is proof. This also solves the metric-free
  case-study problem from a different angle — it gets the number said publicly by the
  party with standing to say it.
- **Pick a topic that demonstrates judgement.** The differentiated asset is the
  one-big-thing report's stance — what we would build, and explicitly **what we would
  refuse to build.** A session built around refusal is contrarian, memorable, and
  demonstrates exactly the judgement a design-led studio sells. "How AI can transform
  hospice documentation" is the opposite: it is what every vendor on the SEO noise floor
  in `03` §1 already says.
- **Have the follow-up built before the webinar, not after.** Every registrant is a
  `/one-thing` input. The pipeline runs a source-traced report on a domain for `Est.`
  $2.84 in 11–13 minutes (measured 2026-09-04, `00-status.md`). A webinar that produces
  125 registrants and no follow-up machine is a waste; the machine already exists.

**Action:** contact Hospice News and Home Health Care News advertising for webinar pricing
and the next available slots. This is a booking conversation that can happen before
Decision #1 lands, because the topic is chosen later.

### 2.2 Prime Health Innovation Challenge — the answer to "pitch competition"

Do not build one. Judge theirs.

The [Prime Health Innovation Challenge](https://www.primehealthco.com/challenge) is a
Colorado institution: annual since 2014, powered by the Colorado Health Foundation, a
three-round programme running **April–October** and concluding in a live pitch competition
where finalists compete for prize funding and **the chance to pilot with a safety-net
partner**. It has supported 45+ pilots across Colorado and California and convened
hundreds of healthcare and technology leaders as judges and mentors.

**Why judging beats hosting.** Judges and mentors are provider, payer, policy, business
and technology executives — meaning the room is Colorado safety-net leadership. That is
not adjacent to the target list, it *is* the target list: **Carina Health Network and
Colorado Access are both Tier A in `11` and both safety-net organisations.** Judging puts
Calvin or Jeff in a working relationship with those executives, in a high-status
non-vendor role, at zero customer-acquisition cost. Running an in-house competition would
cost more, reach fewer of the right people, and read as a vendor promotion.

**Timing, and it matters:** as of 2026-09-08 applications for both companies and judges
are **closed** for the 2026 cycle. **Target the 2027 cycle** — watch for applications to
open ahead of the April start, and get on Prime Health's radar before then via membership
and the Innovation Summit (`11` §3 already flags Prime Health as the highest-value single
membership in Colorado).

### 2.4 Which other sectors' lists to borrow — and the test that decides

The instinct to widen beyond hospice is right; there are three or four more lists worth
renting. But the sector is the wrong unit to reason about, and picking by sector is how
this goes wrong. **The test is not "is this healthcare" or even "is this in our wedge." It
is: does this list's audience own care operations, and does one of our four proof points
map to what they do?**

That produces two distinct failure modes, and the first one is counterintuitive enough to
be worth the space.

**Failure mode 1 — right sector, wrong audience.** Dialysis is squarely in the wedge.
Rendevor maps to it, and Strive, DaVita, US Renal Care and Innovative Renal Care are all
already on the lists in `03` and `11`. But almost every obvious dialysis channel reaches
**clinicians and researchers, not operators**:

| Channel | Audience | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| American Society of Nephrology | Nephrologists and kidney researchers | Skip — wrong buyer |
| National Kidney Foundation Spring Clinical | Clinicians; CE-focused sessions | Skip — wrong buyer |
| Renal Physicians Association | Renal physicians. *Does* sell "Industry Sponsored Webinars" | Skip — sells to you, but the room is clinicians |
| Annual Dialysis Conference | Clinical dialysis audience | Skip — wrong buyer **and** wrong price: published tiers run $35k for swag, $40k conference app, $50k welcome reception, $100k Elite Tier 1 |

RPA is the trap in that table: it will happily sell a sponsored webinar, and the audience
is still wrong. Buying it would look like progress and produce nothing.

**Where the dialysis *operator* audience actually is** — these are the ones to approach:

- **NRAA / Renal Healthcare Association** — the cleanest fit found. Runs "Webinar
  Wednesdays" for members, with topics that are explicitly administrative and operational
  (QAPI, integrated care, water treatment, cost reports, CDC NHSN reporting, advocacy)
  rather than clinical, and it has corporate sponsor tiers. **This is the dialysis
  equivalent of Hospice News.**
- **Renal Exchange — Industry Spotlight** — a sponsored-webinar programme whose back
  catalogue runs on reimbursement, coverage, anemia management and accreditation. Business
  topics, sponsor integration built in.
- **Nephrology Business Leadership University** — explicitly branded around nephrology
  *business* leadership. `(verify)` whether a formal sponsored-webinar product exists, or
  whether the play is event sponsorship.

**Specialty pharmacy — the same pattern, and NASP is the answer.** The HPS "5x faster
medication approvals" number is SeeSaw's strongest proof and specialty pharmacy is where
it lands hardest. **NASP** runs sponsor-presented webinars on operator topics — specialty
pharmacy benchmarks, "reevaluating the specialty pharmacy operating model" — with Pfizer,
Cencora, Cold Chain Technologies and Fairview Pharmacy Solutions as precedent sponsors.
Those are executive topics, not dispensing-pharmacist topics. `(verify)` Asembia, Drug
Channels Institute and Specialty Pharmacy Continuum — all plausible, none confirmed
operator-facing from the sources read.

**Failure mode 2 — wrong sector entirely, which is where wearables sit.** Consumer
wearables and digital fitness fail all three filters. The buyer is a consumer product or
device company, not a care operator. The problem is engagement, retention and consumer app
UX, not clinical workflow. And none of the four proof points map — Diet ID is the nearest
and it is still a nutrition assessment tool, not a device. It is also a market where
consumer product studios compete hard and SeeSaw has no differentiated claim. Borrowing
that list would fill the funnel with the wrong conversations, which is worse than an empty
funnel because it costs the same and teaches the wrong lesson about what works.

**But there is a defensible version of the same idea, and it is worth naming:** not
wearables, but **the operational workflow around remote monitoring data**. Who reviews the
alert, what happens when a threshold trips, how the care team acts, what the reviewer
queue looks like. That is care operations, the proof points map cleanly, and it is already
visible in the target list — cliexa is built on it, InnovAge names RPM in its stated
direction, and DaVita's home-dialysis "connected cyclers" are exactly this problem at
scale. It also lands directly on the SB 26-189 human-review duty in `11` §1. **Sell that
through the care-operations channels above, never through device or wearables channels** —
same subject matter, completely different room.

**One structural opportunity worth exploiting.** Hospice News and Home Health Care News
sit alongside **Skilled Nursing News** and **Behavioral Health Business** in the same
publisher — Aging Media, now operating under **Arrowfly**, itself the new name for WTWH
Media (40+ B2B brands across several industries). Four adjacent ICP lists, one commercial
relationship. Do not buy a single webinar as a one-off: **negotiate the portfolio**, and
ask specifically about their "Clara" reporting product, which they market as giving
partners visibility into **lead quality** rather than just registration counts. Lead
quality is the metric that matters under §8; if it is real, it is worth more than a
discount.

**Two more lists worth a look once the first webinar has run:**

- **National PACE Association** — small, precise, and InnovAge plus Colorado PACE (a Care
  Synergy affiliate) are both already on the `11` list. A tiny audience, but almost every
  member is an ICP.
- **Value-based care and ACO channels** (NAACOS and similar) — reaches the care-management
  buyer, which is where Carina and Colorado Access sit. `(verify)` sponsorship products.

**What to skip in this category, stated plainly:** clinical societies in any sector (wrong
buyer, however good the sector), consumer wearables and digital fitness (wrong buyer and
no proof mapping), generic health IT such as HIMSS or Becker's (the buyer is a
health-system CIO, above SeeSaw's band, and the price reflects it), and generic "digital
health" audiences (founders and investors, not operators).

## 3. Tier 2 — owned formats, small and high-conversion

### 3.1 Executive roundtables, 8–12 people. Highest conversion of anything here.

Dinner or half-day roundtable, Denver and Austin, 8–12 operators, no pitch. This is
already a named workstream (`00-status.md`: "Austin events & exec dinners", Jeff, not
started) and it is the format with the best conversation-per-unit-effort ratio on this
list.

**The thing that makes it work is co-hosting with someone who brings the room.** SeeSaw
does not yet have a list that fills a room; several of the partners in `11` §3 do:

- **Revelstoke Capital** (Denver) — a portfolio-operator roundtable on the Revelstoke
  Frontier AI mandate. Delahanty and Lowder were hired to drive exactly this agenda across
  portfolio companies, so a session that helps them do it is a favour, not a favour asked.
- **vcfo** (Austin, `03` §2) — their CFO clients greenlight the budgets.
- **Prime Health** (Denver) — a safety-net-focused session.

**For the Denver edition, the topic is already written.** SB 26-189's ADMT duties land
2027-01-01 (`11` §1), and every Colorado operator that deployed AI into a decision path
has a reviewer queue and an explanation screen to build. That is a dated, concrete, local
problem — a legitimate reason to convene people rather than a pretext, which is what
separates a roundtable people attend from one they ignore. It does not travel outside
Colorado, which makes it right for a room and wrong for a national webinar.

### 3.2 Office hours — real, but sequence them correctly

Open office hours work when there is already an audience to draw from and fail as an empty
room when there is not. **Do not start here.** Run them *after* the first webinar produces
a registrant list, aimed at that list, and scoped narrowly — "bring one workflow you think
should be automated, leave with a written opinion on whether it should be" is a promise
worth an hour; "ask us anything about AI" is not.

Low cost, low risk, genuinely useful for staying warm with people who are not ready to
buy. Just not a starting move.

## 4. The unfair advantage: the pipeline you already built

The most under-used asset here is not a content format, it is `tools/exposure` and the
`/one-thing` skill. It produces a source-traced research report on any company from its
domain alone, with a claim register where every numeral carries a source, for `Est.` $2.84
in 11–13 minutes (measured 2026-09-04). Almost nobody selling into this market can do that
at that cost, and it changes what content can be.

**Two ways to use it that no competitor can copy cheaply:**

- **The category report, published; the company reports, private.** Run the pipeline
  across a defined set — say the Colorado care-operations list in `11` — and publish the
  *aggregate*: what the category has actually deployed, where the unbuilt workflow
  consistently sits, what the January SB 26-189 date implies. Then use each individual
  report as the dated, specific reason to contact that company. **The content and the
  outreach become the same artifact.** Never publish an individual company's report or
  name a company's weaknesses publicly — that is a trust violation that would cost more
  than the content earns, and it is the one hard line in this section.
- **Run it live.** At a roundtable or on a webinar, offer to run the report on any
  attendee's company and send it over. That is a demonstration of the product rather than
  a description of it, it is unusually generous for the cost, and it produces exactly the
  artifact that starts a sales conversation.

## 5. On giving away build hours

The instinct — put the actual work in front of people rather than talking about it — is
right. The mechanic as stated carries two real risks.

**It selects for the wrong companies.** A free-build-hours prize is most attractive to
organisations that cannot fund the work, which is the opposite of the qualification the
pipeline is meant to do. Expect applicants who need a build and have no budget for the
second one.

**It prices the pod down.** The positioning is premium and design-led, and the ladder in
`03` §1 ($20–25k roadmap → $40–50k/mo pod) is explicitly a differentiator against the
cost-arbitrage tier. Giving hours away teaches the market that the hour is the unit and
that the unit is discountable. Discounting delivery is the one thing that cannot be undone
later.

**The better version: give away the Roadmap.** Award the paid AI Production Roadmap (`04`)
rather than build hours. It qualifies the winner for budget rather than selecting against
it, it demonstrates the actual first-sale product, and the Roadmap's existing job is to
sell the pod — so the prize does the downstream work by design. Same generosity, correct
unit, no damage to delivery pricing.

**And award it inside someone else's competition** (§2.2) rather than running your own.
Borrowed audience, high-status framing, Colorado provider executives in the room, a
fraction of the effort.

## 6. What to skip, and why

Named explicitly so these do not get re-proposed each quarter:

- **A regular company webinar series on your own list.** Without borrowed distribution
  this is a 6-person call. Revisit after §2.1 has run twice and there is a list to invite.
- **A podcast.** Long payback, high production cost, and it competes with the founders'
  scarcest resource — their time in first sales calls, which converts far better.
- **High-volume SEO content.** `03` §1 is unambiguous: the "healthcare AI development
  company" SERP is a listicle war among offshore content machines. Do not enter it.
  (Separately, the DataForSEO keyword pull in `02` is stale as of 2026-07-22 and must be
  re-pulled before any SEO spend at all.)
- **A newsletter, for now.** Only worth it once §2.1 and §3.1 have produced a list of
  people who asked to hear from you. A newsletter is a way to keep an audience, not to get
  one.
- **General startup pitch competitions and demo days.** Wrong audience — founders and
  investors, not care-operations buyers. Prime Health is the exception precisely because
  its judges are providers.

## 8. Measuring it

One rule, consistent with the rest of the plan: **count qualified conversations, not
registrations.** A webinar with 300 registrants and no qualified conversation failed; one
with 40 registrants and 5 real conversations hit the quarter.

Track per activity: qualified conversations booked, opportunities created, and cost per
qualified conversation. Do not report registrations, impressions or followers as outcomes
— under the frame in §1 they are inputs at best, and treating them as results is how a
$45k/mo studio drifts into optimising for a metric that cannot pay for a pod.

Per the never-invent-a-metric rule: **the registrant and attendance figures in §2.1 are
Hospice News's own published averages, not a forecast for SeeSaw.** Everything downstream
of them in this document is arithmetic on their numbers, explicitly labelled as such, and
should not be quoted to anyone as a projection.
